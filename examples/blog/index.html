Essay

Frameworks have too many degrees of freedom

MA Marcelo A. · 8 min read

There are, by a conservative count, several hundred valid ways to centre a div in a modern utility framework. Every one of them works. That is the problem.

Statistical choice is not reasoning

A language model asked to centre a div does not deliberate. It samples from the distribution of things that looked like centred divs in its training data. When the distribution is wide, the output is inconsistent — not wrong, just different every time. Consistency is the thing design systems exist to provide, and it is precisely the thing a wide vocabulary destroys.

Canon's answer is not a better utility set. It is a smaller one.

Closing the vocabulary

Six spacing values. Seven text sizes. Four radii. Three shadows. Seven layouts. When a dimension has six options rather than sixty, choosing correctly stops being a matter of taste and becomes a matter of reading the list.

If something does not have a token, it does not exist.

Intent over implementation

The second move is to describe what a thing is, not how it renders. data-layout="sidebar" carries meaning that survives a redesign. A stack of flex utilities carries only the redesign.

What this costs

Expressiveness, obviously. You cannot build every design in Canon. That is the trade, made deliberately: a framework that says no is a framework whose output you can predict.
